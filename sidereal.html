Scythe of Sidereal

Role: Developer

Scythe of Sidereal is an action-packed, wave-based combat game set in eerie, cursed locations. Players control Scylax, a mysterious exorcist with the ability to wield a scythe in both melee and ranged combat. The game unfolds over three intense waves, each more challenging than the last.

Scylax, cloaked in a shroud of mystery and power, must eliminate demonic beings to achieve godhood. The gameplay emphasizes fast-paced combat, where strategic movement and relentless attacks are key to surviving the overwhelming hordes of enemies.

Key Features

  • Dynamic Combat: Wield Scylax’s scythe as a versatile weapon—slashing through enemies at close range or launching it as a projectile for devastating long-range attacks.
  • Progressive Difficulty: Each wave introduces stronger and more numerous enemies, pushing players to adapt and strategize on the fly.
  • Haunted Environments: Battle through atmospheric and cursed locales like graveyards, haunted manors, and catacombs, each offering unique challenges.
  • Art & Audio Direction: In collaboration with sound designers from Berklee College of Music, the game features a cinematic, eerie soundtrack to enhance exploration, with upbeat combat music inspired by Luigi’s Mansion and Earthbound.

Technical Details

  • Engine: Unity 2D
  • Language: C#
  • Development Timeline: Vertical Slice, Alpha, Beta, Release Candidate (October - December)

Development Team:

The project is developed by a small, focused team, including myself and another developer from USC, alongside talented sound designers from Berklee, who bring the haunted environments to life through a rich and immersive soundscape.

Development Role:

As a core developer, I implement the game's combat mechanics, enemy AI, and progression systems. As an artist, I design the main menu, HUD, cutscenes, enemies, and player animations. I collaborate closely with my fellow USC developer, Riki Lopez, on gameplay features and with Berklee sound designers to integrate audio that complements the game’s visual and interactive elements. Additionally, I help drive the iterative design process by gathering feedback from early playtests and fine-tuning the gameplay experience for balance and engagement.
